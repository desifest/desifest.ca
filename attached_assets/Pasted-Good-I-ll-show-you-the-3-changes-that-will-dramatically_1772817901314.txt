1. Fix the Title Structure (Most Important)

Right now your title is good for humans, but Google likes slightly clearer search language.

Your title:
Indie Artists and the Social Media Challenge

Better version:

Why Independent Artists Struggle With Social Media Reach

or

Why Social Media Is Getting Harder for Independent Artists

Why this matters:

People don’t search
“indie artists social media challenge”

They search:
	•	why musicians struggle with social media
	•	social media for independent artists
	•	how artists grow on social media

Small wording change = much more search traffic.

⸻

2. Add One “Search Paragraph” Early

Google looks for clear context early in the article.

You want one paragraph like this in the first 150 words.

Example:

Independent artists today face a new challenge. Social media platforms once helped musicians reach audiences directly, but organic reach has dropped dramatically across Instagram, TikTok, and YouTube. Many artists now struggle to grow their audience online even when they consistently release music and post content.

This helps Google understand:
	•	topic
	•	keywords
	•	context

But readers still feel like it’s natural.

⸻

3. Add Internal Links (Google Loves This)

Every blog should link to 2–3 other pages on your site.

Example in the article:

When independent artists build community beyond algorithms, real opportunities appear. Platforms like DESIFEST have helped emerging performers connect with audiences and industry professionals in ways that social media alone cannot.

Link “DESIFEST” to:

https://desifest.ca

Or link to:
	•	open mic pages
	•	artist pages
	•	festival pages
	•	future blog articles

This signals to Google that your site is an ecosystem.

That increases ranking power.

Bonus Trick (Almost Nobody Does This)

At the bottom of the blog, add a related articles section.

Example:

Related Reading

Google loves topic clusters.

It tells Google:

“This site is an authority on this topic.”
